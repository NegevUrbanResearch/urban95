Your map, at a glance

Pick a scale and a map measure. Urban95 uses a three-step status scale; Amenities Focus shows citywide percentile rank. Show gaps is available in Amenities Focus.

  • Building, Neighborhood, or City

    Building — Tap or click the map to snap to the nearest home. Actual building footprints show their own status; the categorical surface behind them is inferred from nearby buildings where a direct building sample is absent.

    Neighborhood — Click a neighborhood for charts and stats.

    City — Opens a city sidebar with citywide KPIs and neighborhood comparison. Click a neighborhood on the map or Amenities Focus ranking to focus it; use Open neighborhood for details. Amenities Focus also offers score distribution and optional Show gaps modes for its percentiles. Urban95 remains grouped by named status.

  • Walking time & filter

    Choose 5, 10, or 15 minutes in Amenities Focus. In Urban95, walking-time and amenity filtering controls are hidden because Urban95 uses a fixed methodology.

  • Urban95 and Amenities Focus score

    Urban95 summarizes Environmental Quality, Nature, Play, Safety & Mobility, and Family Services as named statuses. Amenities Focus uses a wider set of amenity types with a simpler POI-based index, making it useful when you want to compare a chosen walking time or amenity filter.

A collaboration between Urban95 and NUR under the “Cities for Children” initiative — exploring how reachable everyday services and child-relevant features are from homes across Beer Sheva.

Status methodologies

1. Urban95: Each direct indicator is classified as Disappointing, Functioning, Thriving, or Unknown. Categories and the five-category overview use equal arithmetic means of direct child attainment values, never averages of status labels. The resulting means are classified as follows: below 0.25 is Disappointing; 0.25 through below 0.75 is Functioning; and 0.75 or higher is Thriving. Unknown propagates strictly: an Unknown required child makes its category Unknown, and an Unknown category makes the overview Unknown. The overview remains categorical. In Building mode, actual building footprints sit over a categorical surface inferred from nearby buildings where direct samples are absent.

Shade (Environmental Quality): Uses Beer Sheva BDAR summer_SI from the Derech Tzel Spatial Shade Index (SI, not SAI; values are not recalculated). Each building gets a 300 m area-weighted mean summer_SI around the building footprint (near-edge buffer), then stored/displayed summer_si is rounded to 1 decimal place with standard half-up ties before status classification and output (0.15 → 0.2, 0.35 → 0.4). The shade map layer shows continuous 0–1 SI with the official interpretation bands: <0.10 severe lack, 0.10–<0.20 significant lack, 0.20–<0.40 needs improvement, 0.40–<0.60 good shade, and ≥0.60 excellent shade. The Urban95 shade indicator uses project-defined direct status thresholds on that rounded building SI: <0.20 = Disappointing, 0.20–<0.40 = Functioning, ≥0.40 = Thriving. Derech Tzel shading metrics guide (PDF).

2. Amenities Focus: Uses the full points-of-interest classification: the index is amenity counts plus one-quarter weight per tree and per street light in range. This is useful for broader type coverage.
